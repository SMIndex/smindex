### The dominant condition, checked against reality

`weekend` is **genuine, not a defect.** Ten examples, all real:

| Blocked at (UTC) | Weekday | Blockers |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-09-05 00:00 | **Saturday** | weekend, spread_unavailable |
| 2026-09-05 00:01 | Saturday | weekend, spread_unavailable |
| 2026-09-05 00:02 | Saturday | weekend, spread_unavailable |
| 2026-09-05 00:03 | Saturday | weekend, spread_unavailable |
| 2026-09-05 00:04 | Saturday | weekend, spread_unavailable |
| 2026-09-04 10:30 | Friday | macro_event — exactly 120 min before the 12:30 UTC event |
| 2026-09-04 10:31 | Friday | macro_event — 119 min before |
| 2026-09-04 10:32 | Friday | macro_event — 118 min before |
| 2026-09-04 05:34 | Friday | oracle_stale |
| 2026-09-04 06:40 | Friday | oracle_stale |

Both time conditions are correct and in UTC, with **no timezone error**:

- The weekend window opens at exactly **2026-09-05 00:00 UTC**, and 2026-09-05
  **is a Saturday**; it closes Sunday 12:00. That is the doc's rule.
- The macro window runs **10:30 → 13:00 UTC** around the real **12:30 UTC**
  event on Friday 2026-09-04 — precisely −30 min to +2 h.
- The macro calendar holds only **two** events for the whole period
  (2026-09-04 12:30 and 2026-10-01 14:00). Thin, but not wrong.

So the market genuinely was in the blocked condition. **No gate threshold
changed.**

### The defect is a different condition — `spread_wide` could never fire

`spread_unavailable` appears on **100.0% of 33,166 rows**. Tracing it:
`scheduler.py` called `regime_mod.evaluate(...)` **without `spread` or
`median_24h_spread`**. Both default to `None`, so `spread_block()` returned
`None` every time and one of the gate's four hard conditions has never once
been able to fire.

The data was not missing. `hl_ws._on_book` computes `best_bid` and `best_ask`
on every 5-second sample and **threw them away** after taking the mid.

**Fixed (D-108), no threshold touched:**

1. `strat_book_5s.spread` — migration v22, nullable so pre-existing rows stay
   honestly unknown.
2. `hl_ws._on_book` persists `best_ask - best_bid`.
3. `Scheduler._spread_inputs()` supplies the latest spread and its 24h median,
   withholding the median until **≥60 samples** so the gate reports
   `spread_unavailable` while it is genuinely unknown rather than guessing.
4. `evaluate()` receives both.

Verified live after deploy: 121 samples per coin, **BTC mean spread $1.00
(≈1.3 bps), ETH $0.10 (≈0.4 bps)**, and the newest regime rows read
`blockers: []` — the first rows in the table's history without
`spread_unavailable`.

Tests: `tests/test_followup_inputs.py` — the pure rule (`>2×` is wide, exactly
`2×` is not), the "unavailable only when really unavailable" contract, and the
UTC weekend/macro boundaries pinned to the real timestamps above.

## 2. M4 CHoCH detector — no defect, and yesterday's classification was wrong

### The candidate list is not empty

180 days of 4h candles, 2026-03-14 → 2026-09-10, 1,081 candles per coin:

| | BTC | ETH |
|---|---:|---:|
| Swings confirmed | 301 (156 high / 145 low) | 309 (157 / 152) |
| Trend label: up / down / range candles | 316 / 270 / 495 | 337 / 277 / 467 |
| BOS events | 83 | 73 |
| **CHoCH events** | **25** | **27** |
| Plain-rule candidates (independent scan) | 72 | 68 |

**52 CHoCH events across both coins — roughly one every 3.5 days.**

First BTC events: 2026-03-21 23:59 down @69,344 · 2026-03-26 07:59 down @70,538
· 2026-03-30 03:59 up @67,096 · 2026-04-12 03:59 down @72,500 · 2026-04-15
07:59 down @73,800.

The independent plain-rule scan finds more candidates (72/68) than the detector
emits (25/27) because `broken_low` / `broken_high` and the `override` correctly
de-duplicate repeated closes through the same swing — the second close below an
already-broken low is not a second change of character. That is right, not a
miss.

**Conclusion: no defect in swing confirmation, trend labelling or the CHoCH
rule. Nothing was changed.**

### What was added instead

A regression test built from **real candles**, as asked: 60 BTC 4h bars ending
just after the CHoCH at **2026-03-26 07:59 UTC on the 70,538 swing low**
(`tests/fixtures_btc4h_choch.json`). It asserts the BOS precedes it and the
CHoCH is detected at that exact level, so a future change cannot silently break
the detector. Two supporting tests pin `trend_label` (needs two ascending highs
**and** lows) and the rule that a close below the last swing low in a `range`
label is a BOS, never a CHoCH.

### M4 funnel over 180 days

| Step | BTC | ETH | Both | Per 30 days |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| CHoCH events | 25 | 27 | **52** | 8.67 |
| …with a prior extended 4h trend | 15 | 12 | 27 | 4.50 |
| …with a 1h BOS confirmation within 72 h | 14 | 12 | 26 | 4.33 |
| **Completed M4 setups** | **14** | **12** | **26** | **4.33** |

### Correction to the 2026-09-11 audit

Yesterday M4 was classified **UNREACHABLE** in Section D on the basis of four
days containing no CHoCH. Over 180 days the condition occurs 52 times and
produces 26 completed setups. **M4 is MARKET, not UNREACHABLE**, and it should
be removed from the Section D decision list. Four days was simply too short a
window to classify anything, which is the more general lesson.

### An incident I caused, and what it cost

Adding the column with a bare `ALTER TABLE` queued behind a pooled connection
that had been **idle-in-transaction for 79 minutes**. MySQL's metadata-lock
queue is FIFO, so the queued ALTER then blocked **every read and insert** on
`strat_book_5s`: the book, OI and taker feeds stopped for about 26 minutes and
app queries piled up to 1,500 s of wait.

Recovery, in the order that matters: kill the ALTER first (that alone unblocks
the queue), then the idle transaction, then re-run as
`SET SESSION lock_wait_timeout=10; ALTER … ALGORITHM=INSTANT` so it either
succeeds immediately or fails harmlessly. Afterwards every pooled connection
still holding the old definition raised `(1412, 'Table definition has changed')`
until the services were restarted — and each worker restart re-runs a full
multi-minute candle backfill before the websocket subscribes, so the feed was
down for the backfill too. Recorded as **D-112**: on this box a schema change to
a hot table costs roughly ten minutes of feed downtime and must be done
deliberately, not mid-investigation.
